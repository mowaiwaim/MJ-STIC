Shang--Zhang two-observation controller for Example 3
======================================================

Run run_shang_comparison in MATLAB R2024a with bw_parameters.m, bw_paths.m
and simulate_bounded_wait.m from the numerical supplement on the path.
Default run_shang_comparison applies the manuscript's H2=0.45 communication
constraint. The optional run_shang_comparison(false) independently calibrates
and evaluates the untruncated rule; its files are in uncapped/.
All generated tables are written to outputs under this script's folder.

Source
------
B. Shang and J.-E. Zhang (2025), Stability of nonlinear stochastic systems
with delayed impulses under self-triggered impulsive control, AIMS Mathematics
10, 20368--20384. DOI 10.3934/math.2025910.
https://www.aimspress.com/aimspress-data/math/2025/9/PDF/math-10-09-910.pdf
Equations (18) and (26) prescribe a state-dependent observation time followed
by a second wait computed using both observations. Equation (26) and its
preceding text specify pi(x)=0.15+0.10 exp(-100||x||), theta=2 and
breve_ell=0.01; the published example uses ell=0.5 and its own quadratic
matrix D. We retain pi, theta and breve_ell and use Q=e' M e from Example 3
in both observations. The sole tuned coefficient is ell. At reference s
(entry to mode 2 or immediately after a pulse), store Q_s. At u=s+pi(e(s))
read Q_u. The deadline from (18) is

    t_next = u + max(0,(ell + log(Q_s/Q_u))/(2+0.01)).

For the bounded implementation replace this deadline by min(t_next,s+0.45).
This is imposed by the comparison network. It is an adaptation of (18).
The intermediate observation always occurs before the cap unless a switch
cancels the plan. Neither a probe nor choosing a deadline resets the
certificate clock; only executed pulses or mode changes reset it.

The positive part implements the infimum over t>u in (18), so a nonpositive
second wait gives a pulse at u. If that occurs, the measurement at u is
reused for the new reference after applying the known reset. The table
records such coincidences. Every pulse multiplies all three errors by 0.5.
A switch to mode 1 cancels a pending intermediate observation or pulse;
a new visit to mode 2 starts a fresh reference. Ties prioritize switching.
Only mode 2 uses this scheduler, and mode 1 retains the original pinning law.

Admissibility and interpretation
--------------------------------
The intermediate observation wait is strictly greater than 0.15 for finite
input (numerically at least 0.15) and at most 0.25. This is a lower bound
on the time from one reference to an executed pulse in the same visit.
The original rule has no finite upper bound on the whole wait: when Q_u
is positive and very small compared with Q_s, the logarithmic term grows.
The bounded adaptation keeps every pending age at most 0.45.
The mean-square clock-certificate argument for this network extends to this
implementation because each deadline is adapted, actual pulses retain the
same all-node jump and scalar contraction, ages lie in [0,0.45], Markov
switches retain their cancel/restart precedence, and the storage is
continuous at probes. The plant and certificate matrices are unchanged.
The resulting bound uses the Example 3 certificate, not the theorem from
Shang and Zhang. The uncapped adaptation has no such verification.
Intermediate observations introduce no generator/jump term.
Mode switches can cancel a previously scheduled action. Both energies are
tested for strict positivity at every actual intermediate observation.

In the original stochastic system (1) the drift has no delayed state, and
the delay is in the jump. One sufficient condition in Assumption 1 is
E[L V]<=theta E[V]. For the numerical adaptation the actual reset yields
Q_plus/Q_minus=0.25. The design search uses 0.501<=ell<=1.35, satisfying
2*0.25<ell and ell+2*0.01*0.25<log(4) for every value. These reproduce the
scalar waiting and contraction restrictions for (18) with the
jump-delay weight set to zero and a current-state multiplier 0.25. The original continuous-flow and switching
assumptions have not been proved for Example 3, whose drift contains a delay
and whose modes cancel and restart the scheduler. No convergence theorem or rate
from the source paper is asserted for either adaptation.

Experimental protocol
---------------------
All coefficients, delay, noises, initial histories and event-aligned
Euler--Maruyama stepping are taken unchanged from Example 3. Both controllers
reuse the same entering/executed event state to initialize the clock. A full
error snapshot is counted for each reference and each successfully reached
intermediate observation; any same-instant reuse is counted once. Cancelled
observations scheduled in the future do not cost a snapshot. Probe and pulse
cancellations are reported separately. Actuation counts are three times
the count of executed pulses. A snapshot denotes acquisition of all three
errors. The base step is 0.005 on [0,8] (1601 grid samples).

The 256 independent pilot paths (seed 512091, mode seed 512092) supply the
actual MJ-STIC pulse target. The bracket endpoints and 12 bisection steps
are tested solely by average pulse count; the observed nearest count wins,
with the first candidate selected in case of ties. Ell is frozen before
opening the 384 test paths (seed 73129, mode seed 73130). The pilot table
records every tried value and the winning row. The other tables retain all
test-path outcomes, the mean error curve on the original time grid and the
summary. Neither pilot errors nor test statistics are used in selection.


The bounded summary reports both cap shares (among completed two-sample
plans, including later cancellations, or among executions alone), and extrema
of planned and executed waiting times on the test set. For an entry canceled
before the probe no full two-sample deadline has yet been computed.
